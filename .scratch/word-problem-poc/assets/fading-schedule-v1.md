# How the guidance fades across the problem set (v1, superseded)

**Superseded on 2026-10-03** by [fading-schedule.md](fading-schedule.md), after the parent objected to her solving whole problems alone in the notebook. Kept as the record of the first resolution.

Decided in [How does the guidance fade across the problem set?](../issues/08-guidance-fading.md) with the parent, 2026-10-03. Terms are from [CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md). The fully guided problem it starts from is in [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md); the levels and slots are in [problem-set-plan.md](problem-set-plan.md).

Four parts were settled by app practice and research, as the parent asked: the short record moving to paper in Level 3, her picking the diagram, the item-by-item self-check, and missed problems coming back. Sources: [fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) (how learning apps do it) and [fading-evidence.md](fading-evidence.md) (the research). A parallel proposal, [fading-proposal.md](fading-proposal.md), was read alongside.

## In short

- **A faded step becomes a paper step.** The app shows only the step's heading. She writes that part of the write-up in her notebook and taps «Готово». The app shows its model and asks a few yes/no checks tied to the model's lines. The app only takes taps and numbers, so producing the short record or a plan can only happen on paper.
- **Fixed schedule, per level.** Each problem's prompted steps are set in its data. The support doesn't adapt to her mistakes, but a missed problem comes back once, at the end of its level.
- **Backward fading, with the short record moving early.** The actions and answer go to paper first, then the short record and the plan. Decode is the last step the app asks. Learners learn most about the steps that are faded, wherever those steps sit (Renkl, Atkinson & Große 2004), and her teacher requires the short record, so she writes it from Level 3.
- **No worked examples.** The set opens with fully guided problems. Each problem's closing Розбір is its worked example, and she sees it after she has tried.
- **She ends on paper, unaided.** 4.6 and 4.7 are solved on paper with no card. 4.7 is the triangle.

## The schedule

| Problems | Prompted in the app | Paper steps | Built-in hints | She types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.1–1.7** | All eight steps | — | Slot count shown; relations listed; diagram and «?» set for her; «Чому?» at the key idea | Each action's result |
| **2.1–2.4** | Перекажи, Знайти, Відомо, Порівняння, Тип і схема, План | Обчисли, Відповідь | The same | Each action's result |
| **2.5–2.8** | The same | Обчисли, Відповідь | **Slot count hidden**: she decides how many actions, then taps «Готово» | Each action's result |
| **3.1–3.8** | Перекажи, Знайти, Порівняння, Тип і схема | **+ Відомо (the short record), План**, Обчисли, Відповідь | Relations listed; **she picks each relation's diagram and places the «?»**; «Чому?» only after the decode | The final answer, and how many actions her plan has |
| **4.1–4.3** | Порівняння only (4.3 has no comparison, so nothing is prompted) | Everything else, as headings one at a time | «Чому?» after the decode (4.1, 4.2) | The final answer, and how many actions |
| **4.4–4.5** | Nothing | The whole write-up, with the **checklist card** on screen | — | The final answer, and how many actions |
| **4.6–4.7** | Nothing | The whole write-up, **no card** | — | The final answer, and how many actions |

- **Order in Level 3.** Перекажи → Знайти → Порівняння → the short record on paper → Тип і схема → the plan, actions and answer on paper. The decode comes before she writes the record, because the record contains the restated comparison. The restated sentence stays on screen for her to write in.
- **Why decode is the last step the app asks.** Level 3 is where inverted wording is new, so the decode stays prompted through all of it and into the first chains.
- **Why the plan stays prompted through Level 2.** Level 2 is the first time a plan has more than one action. Hiding the slot count from 2.5 is the step between picking a plan and writing one.
- **No big jump at the end.** The short record and the plan both go to paper at 3.1. At 4.1 only Перекажи, Знайти and the diagram follow. Several steps switching to writing at once on the hardest problems is what the research warns against.
- **What she produces herself.** The short record and the plan, from 3.1 (15 problems). The whole write-up, from 4.1 (7 problems).
- **Steps a problem doesn't have stay skipped**, as in the guided flow. A problem with no comparison has no Порівняння.

## Paper steps

- **One at a time, up to 4.3.** The heading appears (for example «Запиши короткий запис»). She writes that part in her notebook and taps «Готово». The app shows its model for that part (all valid plans, for the plan) and asks that part's self-checks. The build may put neighbouring paper steps on one screen.
- **Only her paper steps go in the notebook.** She doesn't copy the parts that are still prompted. Those stay on screen. Copying would be transcription, not recall, and it would make every problem longer.
- **The plan on paper is the «Розв'язання» skeleton**: numbered lines with only their explanations («1) … — швидкість хлопчика за хвилину;»). At Обчисли she fills in the calculations. On screen, the План step builds the same skeleton.
- **Перекажи has no line in the write-up**, so once it isn't prompted (from 4.1) it is only a heading, «Перекажи собі задачу», with nothing to check.
- **From 4.4, the whole problem at once.** The app shows the problem (and the card in 4.4–4.5). She writes the whole write-up, then types the final answer and how many actions she wrote. The Розбір shows the model write-up with every part's self-checks.
- **Paper, not a stylus.** Only the tablet has a stylus, and the app couldn't check a drawing anyway.

## Self-checks

Children overrate their own work. Seeing the model helps, and checking it item by item helps more. Format items alone can be ticked when the content is wrong (see [fading-evidence.md](fading-evidence.md), question 3). So each paper part gets 2–3 yes/no checks that name this problem's own lines, shown beside the model:

| Part | Checks (filled in from the write-up) | From |
|---|---|---|
| Short record | «Шукане — ⟨the «?» line⟩?» · for each comparison: «Порівняння записане від шуканого: «⟨the restated line⟩»?» · «Біля кожного числа є одиниця?» | 3.1 |
| Diagram | «Схема того самого виду: ⟨bars / brace / table⟩?» · ««?» стоїть там, де шукане?» | 4.1 |
| Plan | «Скільки дій у твоєму плані?», a typed number, right if it matches any valid plan · «Остання дія знаходить ⟨the asked quantity⟩?» | 3.1 |
| Actions | «Після кожної дії є пояснення?» | 2.1 |
| Answer | «Відповідь — повним реченням про ⟨the asked quantity⟩?» | 2.1 |

- **After any «Ні»**: «Виправ у зошиті», then «Далі».
- **The checks are templates.** They're filled in from the write-up and the plans, so they need no extra writing per problem. The short record's «?» line and its restated comparison lines need tags in the data.
- **Everything is recorded** against its step, like the guided steps' hints and shown answers: each check's answer, the action count, where a wrong answer went off, and hints she asked for.

## What she types, and what happens on a miss

- **Level 2: each action's result.** The plan is still prompted, so the app knows her plan, and for each action it shows the explanation and takes the result on the keypad. A miss gets hint-then-show: first the action's hint from the data, then the action shown. Mistakes are caught at the action where they happen.
- **From Level 3: the final answer, and how many actions.** The plan is on paper, so the app no longer knows it. The action count is the one objective check on the plan, as Sparx checks written work with a number the student types (see [fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md)). A wrong final answer first gets «Не сходиться. Перевір дії ще раз.» The second time, the app shows the model write-up, and she taps where hers went off: short record, plan or calculation.
- **Two-part answers** (such as 3.3, and 3.7's «Хто … більше і на скільки?») need two entries, or a pick plus a number.

## Missed problems

- **Inside a problem, help comes at once**, as above: hint-then-show on prompted steps and typed results, then the model and "where did it go off". Nearly every app surveyed helps on the missed problem straight away, and feedback that explains beat giving only the answer (see [fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) and [fading-evidence.md](fading-evidence.md), question 4).
- **A problem is missed** when the app had to show its answer: a typed result or the final answer.
- **A missed problem comes back once, at the end of its level**, at her current stage (see Replays). A problem missed in Level 4 comes back after 4.7. If she misses it again, it doesn't come back a third time.
- **Why at the end of the level, not right away.** She has just seen the model, and re-solving a problem straight after its worked solution was no better than restudying it (van Gog et al. 2015). A gap gives spacing. Duolingo brings missed items back at the end of a lesson in the same way. The next problems of the same type are her other retries, and the set already has them.
- **The repeat is checked by her notebook, not the number.** She may remember the answer. The self-checks and the parent reading the page show whether she can actually do it.

## Hints

- **Prompted steps** keep the guided flow's hints, which come after a wrong answer and are tied to the misreading she picked. There is no hint button on them.
- **Paper steps get a «Підказка» button.** The first tap shows the step's self-question, which is that step's line on the checklist card. The second tap shows the model for that part. In 4.6–4.7, «Підказка» brings back the card.
- **Hints are free and recorded.** A cost would push her to guess on menus and skip writing on paper. What a wrong answer costs is the game ticket's call, but asking for help should never cost more than guessing.

## Built-in hints

| Hint | How it fades |
|---|---|
| Slot count in План | Shown in Level 1 and 2.1–2.4; hidden in 2.5–2.8 |
| Relations listed in Тип і схема | Stay as long as Тип і схема is prompted (Levels 1–3) |
| Diagram chosen for her | Levels 1–2. From 3.1, after naming each relation's type, she picks its diagram from the three («Яку схему накреслиш?»), and the app draws the combined diagram. On paper (Level 4) she draws it |
| «?» placed for her | Levels 1–2. From 3.1 the «?» is a chip she places, like the numbers |
| Three-option menus | Stay at three. More options would mean more misreadings to write for every problem |
| «Чому?» | Goes with its step: shown only while the step it follows is prompted. After the decode, through 4.2; after the plan, through Level 2 |

- **Why pick the diagram.** On paper she'll have to choose one, and six types share three diagrams. Schema programs teach naming the type as its own skill, and a grade 6 curriculum has students choose among diagrams once each has been taught. Placing the «?» matters more: an accurate diagram (which bar is longer, where the unknown sits) is what predicts right answers (see [fading-evidence.md](fading-evidence.md), question 2).
- **The bars give nothing away.** The diagram is drawn to scale only after the decode, as in the guided flow, so which bar is longer can't reveal the decode.
- **The pick needs no data.** Each type's diagram is fixed (see [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md)), so the right answer follows from the type.

## The checklist card

On screen, as a panel under the problem text, in 4.4–4.5. Its lines are the paper-step headings from Level 4, stacked. The draft wording is for the parent to review with the data:

1. Прочитай задачу двічі. Перекажи її собі.
2. Що треба знайти? Запиши з «?».
3. Що відомо? Запиши кожне число з одиницями.
4. Є порівняння? Хто більший? Запиши його від шуканого.
5. Назви тип кожного зв'язку. Намалюй схему.
6. План: що знаходить кожна дія? Остання — те, що питають.
7. Розв'язання: «1) … = … (од.) — …;»
8. Відповідь — повним реченням.

Lines 2–4 are the short record her teacher requires.

## Handover screens

At the first problem of each new stage (2.1, 2.5, 3.1, 4.1, 4.4 and 4.6), a short screen says what she now does herself, for example «Тепер дії і відповідь ти записуєш у зошит». How it looks, and whether it's a celebration, is up to the game ticket and the build.

## Replays

This covers missed problems coming back and problems she taps to redo. A replayed problem runs at her current stage. It prompts only the steps that both the replayed problem and her furthest problem prompt. If she's reached 3.4 and replays 1.3, the app prompts Перекажи, Знайти, Порівняння and Тип і схема, and the short record and the plan go on paper. After 4.7, every replay is all paper with no card. A replay never prompts more than the problem did first time, so it needs no extra data.

## What each problem needs in the data

Every problem needs its write-up (the model for paper steps, with the short record's «?» line and restated comparison lines tagged for the self-checks), its other valid plans, its final answer, and the closing screen's checks and key idea. On top of that:

| Problems | Guided-step data |
|---|---|
| 1.1–1.7 | Everything in "What each problem needs in the data" in [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md) |
| 2.1–2.8 | Everything except the Відповідь menu. Each action's hint stays: a miss on a typed result uses it |
| 3.1–3.8 | Text, Retell, Asked, Decode, «Чому?» (after the decode only), Relations, Diagram. No Given menus, plan cards, actions or answer menu |
| 4.1–4.3 | Text with comparison spans, Decode and «Чому?» for 4.1 and 4.2. Nothing for 4.3 |
| 4.4–4.7 | None |

So 15 problems need full or nearly full guided data, 10 need partial data, and 5 need none.

## Not chosen

- **Worked examples first, or example–problem pairs.** The closing Розбір already shows the worked solution after each problem, and pairs would add 7 problems.
- **Fading a step once she's done it right twice.** Every problem would need full guided data, and each run would differ, which makes the trial harder to read. A streak could also fade the decode just before Level 3 makes it hard, and two lucky guesses on a three-option menu happen about one time in nine. Adaptive fading did beat fixed in one study (Salden et al. 2010, cited in [fading-proposal.md](fading-proposal.md)), but its signal was a tutor's model of the quality of high-school students' self-explanations, not a "two right" counter.
- **A fallback to the guided version after a wrong paper answer, or menus coming back after repeated misses.** Same data cost, and no app surveyed does it. Apps add support inside the missed problem, straight away.
- **Holding progress until she gets it right (mastery gating).** With a fixed 30 problems, a gate would loop on problems she has already seen, and apps that gate (MATHia, ASSISTments, IXL) cap it and move the student on anyway. Mastery learning also shows near-zero effects on standardized tests.
- **A missed problem straight away.** She'd be re-solving from memory of the model she just saw.
- **The app filling a faded step in, or the step just disappearing.** She would never produce it.
- **The short record staying in menus until Level 4.** It would have given her only 7 problems of writing the record her teacher requires, and put four steps onto paper at once on the hardest problems.
- **A single «Так» / «Інакше» tap per part.** Children overrate their work, and a whole-model comparison catches less than item-by-item checks.
- **A format checklist without the model.** Format items can be ticked when the content is wrong.
- **Faster or slower schedules.** Paper from 2.5 risked the fun and planning with too few prompted plans behind her. Paper only in Level 4 meant the jump from picking to producing in 7 problems, alongside the hardest ones.
- **A printed card.** It needs a printer, gets lost, and «Підказка» can't bring it back.

## Limits

- **Not tried with her.** The schedule is a guess, built from a suggestion in [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) that was itself untested. The evidence for the three research-settled parts is moderate at best, and mostly from other ages, subjects or tasks.
- **Self-checks are still her own judgement.** The final answer and the action count are the only checks the app makes on paper work.
- **Repeated mistakes aren't flagged to anyone.** Apps that cap their retries tell the teacher (MATHia's report, Zearn's alerts). Here the records stay on the device. Whether the parent sees a list (problems whose answer was shown, steps checked «Ні» twice in a row) is the trial's call.
- **Runs differ only by the repeats.** A missed problem adds one replay at the end of its level. Everything else is the same for every run.
- **The triangle comes last, unaided.** That is the intent, but it's also the hardest problem with the least support. If she can't do it, the record shows which part went wrong, and the parent sees it on paper.
