# How learning apps handle three fading questions

Desk research, 2026-10-03, for the questions raised by [fading-schedule.md](fading-schedule.md). Each claim carries a tag:

- **[V]** I read it in the linked source. For research papers this is sometimes only the abstract, as noted.
- **[S]** I saw it only in a search-result summary, not in the source itself.
- **[I]** My inference. No source says it.

## TL;DR

1. **When does the short record move to paper?** No app I found stages one late handover of a setup step. Blended products (Zearn, ASSISTments, Sparx, Carnegie Learning) put written work on paper from the first lesson, alongside the software. Software tutors that check setup (Cognitive Tutor) make the student build it from early lessons, with feedback on each cell. Paper interventions for word problems (schema-based instruction) give the diagram for a couple of sessions per problem type, then the student draws it. Research on fading finds students learn most about the steps that were faded. This practice favours **handing the short record over earlier (the Level 3 alternative)**.
2. **Choosing the diagram.** Apps mostly choose it for the student. In Thinking Blocks the topic menu decides the model, and the work area has fixed targets. Schema programs make "name the problem type" an explicit early step, and they drill it separately (Pirate Math sorts problems by type every day). A grade 6 curriculum (Illustrative Mathematics) has students choose among tape diagram, table and number line once each one has been taught. This practice favours **the alternative: pick the diagram from Level 3**.
3. **Self-checks of paper work.** Apps that mix paper and screen check only the typed answer (ASSISTments). Or they spot-check that the written work exists (Sparx asks for the answer written under a code). Or a teacher reads the paper later (Zearn, Kumon). Zearn has students check and correct their paper notes against the on-screen lesson, which is close to "same / different". Some word-problem programs add a self-check checklist (GO Solve, Math Explorer). In one study, checking an answer part by part made students less overconfident than comparing it with the whole model. This **supports adding the checklist**, best as part-by-part checks next to the model.
4. **Reacting to mistakes.** Right after a miss, almost every app helps on the same item: another try, a hint, a worked explanation, or smaller step questions. The PoC already does this. When misses repeat, most apps serve a *new* item on the same skill and hold progress until a criterion is met. They always cap it, then move the student on and tell a teacher. Only Duolingo brings the *same* missed item back *later*, at the end of the lesson. Sparx retries it straight away. With a fixed set and no item pool, this supports **(A), missed problems come back, plus an explicit flag for the parent**. It does not support mastery gating (D) or menus coming back (C).

## 1. Handing a setup step from guided input to student production

- **Cognitive Tutor (now MATHia).** Students "construct the Worksheet" themselves. They pick the quantities, label the columns, give units and write the formula row. The tutor checks each entry. The layout changes across lessons: the formula row moves from bottom to top, and later the sheet computes values itself. So the tutor takes over mastered low-level work. It does not hand setup work to the student late. [V] [koedinger.pdf](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/85-412/readings/koedinger.pdf); [KoedingerCorbett2006.pdf](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2006/KoedingerCorbett2006.pdf)
- **Carnegie Learning classroom days.** Students solve similar problems on paper. Teachers connect the computer tools to paper methods. "From the first day all answers must be written in complete sentences." [V] [koedinger.pdf](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/85-412/readings/koedinger.pdf)
- **MATHia workspaces** open with an interactive Step-by-Step example. After that the student does every step, with hints on demand. There is no step-by-step fading schedule. [V] [Carnegie Learning support](https://support.carnegielearning.com/help-center/math/home-connection/mathia-support22/accessing-help-in-mathia/article/ways-to-get-support-when-working-in-mathia/)
- **Zearn.** In each digital lesson, students are "prompted to complete problems in their paper Student Notes … check and correct their work". Each lesson ends with an "un-scaffolded" paper Exit Ticket. Paper production happens daily from the start. [V] [Zearn G3 Course Guide](https://webassets.zearn.org/resources/G03_Course_Guide_Z1.pdf)
- **ASSISTments and Sparx.** All working happens on paper from day one. Only answers go into the app. [V] [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org/blog-posts/year-long-study-on-homework); [Sparx](https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks)
- **Schema-based instruction (SBI; paper, not an app).** "Visual diagrams and checklists are initially provided … these are gradually removed or replaced by student constructed diagrams." [V] [Jitendra summary](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1674664881/wakullaschooldistrictorg/pp51cdh1mjkzyqnumvfc/TeachingMathWithSchema-BasedInstructionSBI.pdf). One study gave each problem type two sessions with the diagram provided, then three without, then mixed problems. [V] [EJ1314840](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1314840.pdf)
- **Thinking Blocks** never hands model building over inside the app. After repeated errors it says "The model has been completed for you." [V] [game script](https://www.mathplayground.com/tb_addition/addition.min.js)
- **Fading research.** In Renkl et al. (2004), where in the solution the faded steps sat did not change learning. Students "learned most about those principles that were faded". The participants were not children. [V, abstract] [Renkl et al.](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/how-fading-worked-solution-steps-works-a-cognitive-load-perspecti/). In the Geometry Cognitive Tutor, adaptive fading beat fixed fading on delayed transfer. [S] [Salden et al.](http://www.cee.uma.pt/ron/Salden%20et%20al.%20-%20The%20Expertise%20Reversal%20Effect%20and%20Worked%20Examples.pdf)
- **What this means.** In the apps, the student produces the setup early. Either the software checks it (Cognitive Tutor), or it is on paper from the start (Zearn, ASSISTments). In this PoC a menu-built short record is recognition, not production. Seven Level 4 problems is a short run of practice writing it, and those problems are the hardest ones. [I]

## 2. Choosing the representation before it is provided

- **Thinking Blocks.** The student picks a category from a menu ("Part Whole - 2 Parts", "Compare - 2 Steps", "Change - Work Backward"). The instructions then name the model ("Build a part whole model") and say "Drag blocks and labels to the targets in the work area." The model type is never the student's call. [V] [addition script](https://www.mathplayground.com/tb_addition/addition.min.js); [multiplication script](https://www.mathplayground.com/tb_multiplication/multiplication.min.js)
- **GO Solve Word Problems (Scholastic, schema-based).** It "presents a different graphic organizer for each problem situation" and teaches one situation at a time. First the words go into the organizer, then the numbers, then the student computes. [V] [white paper](https://teacher.scholastic.com/graphic-organizers/go-solve/pdfs/GSWPwhitepaper.pdf). I could not confirm whether it ever asks the student to pick the organizer.
- **Pirate Math Equation Quest (paper tutoring).** Its RUN routine is "Read; Underline the label…; Name the problem type". Daily "Shipshape Sorting" (from lesson 7) drills naming the type, with the answer on the back of each card. The manual warns that "students often struggle to identify the correct problem type after all three schemas have been introduced." [V] [teacher materials](https://piratemathequationquest.education.utexas.edu/docs/PMEQ-1A-Teacher-Materials.pdf)
- **SBI.** Its four-step checklist starts with "F – find the problem type". The diagram follows from the type. [V] [Jitendra summary](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1674664881/wakullaschooldistrictorg/pp51cdh1mjkzyqnumvfc/TeachingMathWithSchema-BasedInstructionSBI.pdf)
- **Illustrative Mathematics, grade 6 (a curriculum, used on several platforms).** Students first learn double number lines, tables and tape diagrams one at a time. Then in Lesson 16 they "allow students to choose the representation they prefer" and "decide on methods that would make the most sense". [V] [IM 6.2.16](https://im.kendallhunt.com/MS/teachers/1/2/16/index.html)
- **What this means.** The common pattern is: teach each diagram type with the type given, then make the student pick before she draws. In this PoC she already names relation types, so picking the diagram is a small extra step. On paper she will have to pick anyway. [I]

## 3. Self-checks of work the app can't read

- **ASSISTments.** Students do homework on paper and type answers to see if they are right. A wrong answer opens "scaffolding questions" that break the problem into steps. [V] [ASSISTments](https://www.assistments.org/blog-posts/year-long-study-on-homework); [Feng & Heffernan](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~nth/pubs_and_grants/papers/journals/feng_heffernan_JILR.pdf). Written explanations are scored by the teacher. [S] [open response](https://www.assistments.org/individual-resource/how-to-score-open-response-questions-that-are-not-graded-automatically-in-assistments). I found no student self-assessment feature.
- **Sparx Maths.** Each question has a bookwork code. Students write the code, their working and their answer in their book. Now and then a "bookwork check" asks for the answer they gave to an earlier code, "even if it was wrong". [V] [student guide](https://5530880.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/5530880/The%20Student%20Experience%20-%20Sparx%20Maths.pdf); [support](https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks). This checks that the paper work exists, not that it is good.
- **Zearn.** Students check and correct their paper Student Notes against the lesson. The paper Exit Ticket goes to the teacher. [V] [course guide](https://webassets.zearn.org/resources/G03_Course_Guide_Z1.pdf)
- **Kumon Connect.** Human graders mark the work, and students correct it. [S] [Kumon](https://www.kumon.com/resources/how-kumon-connect-works/)
- **Mathspace.** Instead of self-checks, it reads typed or handwritten (iPad) lines and marks every step. [V] [help](https://help.mathspace.co/en/articles/3612642-student-workbook-part-1-basic-features)
- **Anki** is the plain "compare with the model and rate yourself" design. The user reveals the answer, compares and taps Again/Hard/Good/Easy. [V] [manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/studying.html)
- **Checklists in word-problem software.** GO Solve gave "a self-regulating checklist for following the steps". Math Explorer and Fun Fraction put a "Check" or "Have I understood…?" self-question inside each step. [V] [synthesis, EJ1339497](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1339497.pdf)
- **Research on self-scoring.** Students judged their own recalled definitions. Those who checked "each of the individual ideas" in the correct answer were less overconfident than those given the full answer to compare with. The participants were college students. [V, abstract] [Dunlosky et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2010.502239)
- **What this means.** "Same / different" against a whole model is the Anki design, and the study above found students overconfident when judging that way. Yes/no checks per part are more accurate. Format items («?», units, an explanation after each action, a full sentence) are visible marks, so she can check them easily. But they check form, not correctness. A Sparx-style number check fits a taps-and-numbers app, for example "How many actions did you write?" [I]

## 4. Reacting to mistakes

TL;DR: help on the same item at once, then a new item on the same skill, with progress held up to a cap and a human told when the cap is hit. Duolingo alone repeats the missed item itself.

**Patterns.**

- **Immediate help on the same item is universal.** It comes as a retry, a hint, an explanation or step questions. The PoC already gives hint-then-show and one retry on paper answers. [V across rows]
- **Repeated misses mostly lead to a *new* item on the same skill.** IXL, ASSISTments, MATHia, Zearn and Prodigy draw from item pools. Only Duolingo (end of lesson, later lessons) and Sparx (retries, then a variant) bring back the item itself. [V/S]
- **Gating is common, but always capped and handed to a human.** MATHia stops at about 25 problems, ASSISTments at 10 a day, Zearn and Sparx alert the teacher. ASSISTments says its daily cap allows "for teacher intervention". [V]
- **More support next time (C) happens inside the missed item, at once.** Zearn's Boost and ASSISTments' scaffolds work this way. IXL and Prodigy make *later* questions easier, but they have item pools to do it with. I found no app that turns earlier scaffolding back on for later items after misses on a step. [V/I]

**What this means for the PoC.** [I]

- **D does not fit.** A fixed set of 30 has no pool, so gating would mean redoing the same problems. MATHia's cap alone (about 25 problems) is close to the whole set.
- **C copies nothing common** and needs menu data for Level 4.
- **A is the version of "another item on the same skill" this set can afford.** It is also Duolingo's exact pattern. Two caveats: she has seen the model write-up, so the replay partly tests memory, and a gap of a few problems helps with that. Problems missed at 4.6–4.7 have nothing after them, so they come back at the end.
- **B is what apps do after their cap, but they make it explicit.** MATHia reports to the teacher, Zearn sends Tower Alerts, Sparx raises "seek help". The PoC already records every miss. The cheap step is a short list for the parent: problems whose answer had to be shown, and steps marked «Інакше» twice in a row.

## Not addressed, or not found

Khan Academy, IXL, Mathspace (beyond step marking), Bettermarks, Brilliant, Duolingo Math, Prodigy, Matific and Smartick: I found no public material on any of the three questions. They work fully on screen. Photomath shows solution steps but does not grade the student's own work [I]. DragonBox slowly turns picture cards into symbols [S] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DragonBox)), but it uses no paper and no diagram choice. I found no software version of Jitendra's SBI except GO Solve. I found nothing usable on Mathletics or HMH's Bar Models app.

## Where the schedule departs from common practice [I]

- **Nothing reacts to errors.** Almost every app adds help after a wrong answer: ASSISTments scaffolds, Zearn's "Boost" ([course guide](https://webassets.zearn.org/resources/G03_Course_Guide_Z1.pdf)), Thinking Blocks finishing the model. Most also hold progress until mastery: MATHia's knowledge tracing ([koedinger.pdf](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/course/85-412/readings/koedinger.pdf)) and Zearn's "all problems correctly" rule. Here a wrong final answer only leads to the model write-up, and she moves on whatever happens.
- **Paper starts late.** Blended products have paper work from lesson one. Here it starts at 2.1, and the short record only reaches paper at 4.1.
