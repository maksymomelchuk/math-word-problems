## 1. Days and minutes a day

**Recommendation.** Four sittings a week on school days, 15–25 minutes each, as products for her age plan (Smartick 15; DreamBox and Pirate Math 20–30: [fading-revisit-apps.md](fading-revisit-apps.md) §7). The set and its repeats take 16–19 sittings, about 4–5 weeks (calendar in part 2). Then **1–2 free weeks**: no schedule, no reminders. Replays stay open, all on paper, with the solo try offered. Near the end you may offer once: «Хочеш спробувати якусь задачу сама?». The end chat and the "after" note close the trial.

**Why.**

- **It's a short dose, so spread it.** The method's studies ran 7–18 hours: 10 lessons of 45 minutes (Jitendra et al. 2009) and 20 of 50 minutes (Jitendra et al. 2015, [manuscript](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED572835.pdf)); Fuchs et al. 2008, 36 sessions of 20–30 minutes ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2832201)). This set is 3–5 hours, so expect a change in *how she approaches* a problem more than in her marks.
- **Spacing works at her age.** Year 7 pupils who spread practice over three weekly sessions beat those who massed it, on a test a month later ([Emeny, Hartwig & Rohrer 2021](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED611846.pdf)).
- **The free weeks are the delayed reading.** Returning to an activity unasked is the standard measure of intrinsic motivation (Deci 1971, in [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)). Her homework then shows transfer; replays show less, as she may remember the answer (van Gog et al. 2015, in [fading-evidence.md](fading-evidence.md)).

| Alternative | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| Daily, 10–15 minutes (Smartick, Duolingo) | Finer spacing, but only one Level 3–4 problem fits, and it becomes a chore |
| A second pass instead of free weeks | More dose, but re-solving adds little, and boredom muddies *is it interesting* |

## 2. Problems per sitting

**Recommendation.** No problem count. **Don't open a new problem after about 15 minutes**; finish the open one, so a sitting ends with a problem after 15–25 minutes. Each handover (2.1, 2.5, 3.1, 4.1, 4.4, 4.6) opens a sitting, so she meets it fresh and you're there. If she's tired, stop anyway: the open problem resumes, and she finishes it before opening another.

**Estimated calendar**, from each stage's paper steps and action count, anchored on the schedule's guesses (the triangle: 4–6 minutes fully guided, 8–11 on paper). Recheck after week 1.

| Problems | On paper | Minutes each | Per sitting | Sittings | Week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1–1.7 | Nothing | 3–5 | 3–4 | 2 | 1 |
| 2.1–2.8 | Actions, answer | 5–8 | 2–3 | 3–4 | 1–2 |
| 3.1–3.8 | + short record, her own plan | 7–10 | 2 | 4 | 2–3 |
| 4.1–4.5 | Almost everything | 9–13 | 1–2 | 3–4 | 4 |
| 4.6, 4.7 | Everything, or the solo try | 8–14 | 1 | 2 | 5 |
| Repeats | At each level's end | 5–10 | — | 2–3 | — |

With few misses, it's about 3½ weeks. A daily goal in the game layer should count a sitting or one finished problem; option 3's 3 problems a day would rush Level 4 (for [ticket 09](../issues/09-game-mechanics.md)).

| Alternative | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| A fixed count per level (4 / 3 / 2 / 1) | Simple, but one hard problem runs past 30 minutes |
| A 20-minute timer | Predictable, but she stops mid-plan |

## 3. What the parent watches

**Recommendation.** Sit beside her at the first sitting, each handover and each solo try (about 9 sittings). Otherwise stay within earshot and look at her notebook afterwards. **Don't teach.** If she asks: «Спробуй — гра підкаже.» Help only with the device or the notebook format, and note what you wanted to say: it shows where the app's help falls short. A separate trial notebook keeps her write-ups together as evidence. Watch above all whether she reads before tapping: quick guessing ("gaming the system") goes with poorer learning in tutors ([Baker 2007](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/BakerCHI2007Final.pdf)), and two-button checks invite it.

**Observation sheet.**

*Every sitting, one line:*

| Date | Start–end | Problems | Started by (she / reminded / reminded more) | Mood start → end | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

*When watching:*

- Did she read the whole problem before tapping, and reread after a hint? (yes / partly / no)
- Where did an answer have to be shown?
- Notebook: short record with «?» and units, actions with explanations, full-sentence answer?
- What did she say to herself?
- What I wanted to say

*Solo try (4.6, 4.7, replays):*

- Her choice, and any hesitation.
- Her order: short record, diagram, plan line by line? Inverted comparison read right?
- Where she switched, if she did; minutes; mood.
- Then ask: «Розкажи, як ти її розв'язувала.»

*Homework (weekly, before you help):*

- Short record unprompted? Diagram? Actions explained? Inverted wording handled?
- Stuck on reading, what's asked, the plan, or arithmetic?
- The teacher's comment, if any.
- Ask: «З чого ти почала?»

| Alternative | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| Sit beside her every time | You see everything, but she may lean on you |
| Watch only solo tries and homework | Least of your time, but you miss how the handovers land |

## 4. What the PoC shows the parent

**Today** (`poc/src/lib/progress.ts`, `poc/src/screens/ParentView.tsx`), «Для батьків» lists each attempt per problem: start and finish times, the plan, the Тип і схема version, and each hint, shown answer and slip by step, with any wrong sign. No summary, no durations. **The fading build** ([ticket 14](../issues/14-build-fading.md)) adds the repeat queue and records plan lines, results, direction answers, next-step picks, self-checks, «Підказка» taps and the solo-try choice, but doesn't say they're shown.

**Still to build** (one small ticket, blocked by 14):

1. **«Де довелося показати»** at the top: one line per missed problem (a plan line or result shown), by level, saying what was shown, whether it was a slip, and how the repeat went: «3.4 · Кавуни, 5 жовт. — показано дію 1 (знак «−»); повтор — без показу».
2. **Solo tries**: one line per problem played at 4.6, 4.7 or later, with her choice, the outcome ("right", "switched at ⟨step⟩", "wrong answer → step by step") and minutes. In the free weeks you may not be watching.
3. **Minutes** on each finished attempt (pauses included).
4. **«Скопіювати записи»**: the records as text. They live only on the iPad; keep a weekly copy.

**Add to ticket 14:** show its new records in words. **Not needed:** charts, per-type scores, daily totals.

Precedents: Zearn alerts the teacher when a student keeps struggling ([fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md)); Prodigy shows parents where the child "is struggling" ([Prodigy](https://www.prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/parents-guide-to-prodigy)).

| Alternative | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| Build nothing; read the attempt lines | No build, but a long scroll, free-week solo tries are easy to miss, and a reset loses everything |

## 5. Her opinion for *is it interesting*

**When.** Two questions at the end of Levels 1, 2 and 3 (about weekly), and a 10-minute chat after the free weeks, at a relaxed moment, never after a hard problem. Talk, don't hand her a form. Open with: «Мені важливо почути і хороше, і погане: так я зроблю гру кращою. Правильних відповідей тут немає.»

**At the end of a level:**

1. «Як тобі цей рівень?»
2. «Що тобі сподобалось, а що — ні?»
3. After Level 2 only: «Як тобі писати в зошиті поруч із грою?»

**The end chat:**

1. «Розкажи, як тобі було розв'язувати задачі в цій грі.»
2. «Що тобі сподобалось, а що — ні?», then «Що б ти змінила?»
3. «Як би ти розповіла про цю гру подрузі?» (lets her criticise without criticising you)
4. «Як ти зараз розв'язуєш задачі вдома чи в школі?»
5. «Що б ти хотіла робити з цією грою далі?»

Follow up only with «Розкажи більше» or «А ще?», and don't defend the design. Children answer open invitations more fully and accurately than yes/no questions ([Lamb et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3999890/)), and tend to say yes to please an adult ([de Leeuw](https://www.aka.fi/globalassets/tietysti1.fi/awanhat/documents/tiedostot/lapset/presentations-of-the-annual-seminar-10-12-may-2011/surveying-children-and-adolescents_de-leeuw.pdf); both summaries).

**Kept** in the same log, in her words, with the date; at the end, in the verdict ticket.

**Other signals**, from the log and «Для батьків»: who started each sitting; asking for one more, or stopping at the first chance; mentioning it unprompted; choosing the solo try; the days she opened it in the free weeks (attempt dates). What blurs them:

- **Rewards.** Don't trade screen time or treats for sittings: expected tangible rewards lower free-choice interest, more in children, while praise raises it ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)).
- **Novelty.** Gamification's effect dipped after about 4 weeks in one study ([Rodrigues et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6), summary); compare week 1 with week 4.
- **A streak**, if ticket 09 picks one, pulls her back by design.

| Alternative | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| An in-app smiley after each problem ([Fun Toolkit](https://experienceresearchsociety.org/ux-methods/fun-toolkit/)'s Smileyometer) | Children her age do discriminate with it ([Read & Horton 2025](https://academic.oup.com/iwc/advance-article/doi/10.1093/iwc/iwaf016/8131678)), but it's a build and feels like a survey |
| Another adult asks | Less pull to please you, but she may say less |

## What the verdict will need

**Does it help** (your judgement): the "before" note and photos, the log and notes, the «Для батьків» list, and an "after" note under the same headings with 2–3 new photos. Write it after the free weeks, not on the last sitting: learning shows best after a delay, and massed practice leaves learners overconfident (Emeny et al. 2021). Add which steps reached her paper work (decode, short record, plan one action at a time, direction check): that names the core loop.

**Is it interesting**: her words, the signal tally, and her pick in ticket 09.

**The "before" note: write it now, as one paragraph.**

> Before the trial, ⟨date⟩. With a homework word problem, she … (reads it once or twice, computes at once, asks what to do). She writes a short record … (always / when reminded / rarely). Two-step problems: … Three or more steps: … «На … менше від …» wording: … Speed–time–distance and «у … разів»: … She asks me for help about … times a week, mostly about … Her mood with word problems: … Her teacher's recent comments: … I'd say it helped if, by the end, …; and that it didn't if …

Writing that last sentence now keeps hindsight from deciding it later. All minutes above are guesses until week 1 is timed.
