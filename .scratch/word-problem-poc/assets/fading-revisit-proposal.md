# Revisit proposal: guidance that stays step by step to the end

Written 2026-10-03 (AFK) for the reopened [How does the guidance fade across the problem set?](../issues/08-guidance-fading.md), as the starting point for the parent's new grilling. **It is a proposal, not a decision.** Nothing here has been tried with her. Terms are from [CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md). A *step* is a step of the routine, and an *action* is a numbered line of «Розв'язання».

The closed schedule is [fading-schedule.md](fading-schedule.md). After running the game mocks ([game-mechanics-mock.html](game-mechanics-mock.html)), the parent wrote:

> "I dont like this mechanics because she need to solve whole word-problem alone in notebook. it should be better way to decompose that problem and solve it step by step."

## Recommendation

The right alternative depends on what the objection is (see [section 1](#1-what-the-parent-objected-to)), so the grilling should settle that first.

**If the objection is to being handed the whole problem (reading 2), take B: paper steps one at a time, to the end.** B keeps the closed schedule and replaces only its last stage:

- **No problem is handed over whole.** Up to 4.7, the app opens one step at a time. She writes that part in her notebook, and the app checks it before opening the next step.
- **From 3.1, the solution is checked action by action, as Level 2 already is.** She writes the plan herself. The app checks it line by line before she computes: after each line in Level 3, and after the whole plan from 4.1. Then it names each action of her plan, she works it out in the notebook, and she types the result. A wrong first action is caught straight away, not at the final answer.
- **The last thing handed over is the order of the steps.** In 4.6–4.7 she picks the next step herself before it opens. The checklist card goes.
- **Everything else stays**: the levels and the 30 slots, the stages and handover points (2.1, 2.5, 3.1, 4.1, 4.4, 4.6), backward fading with the decode last, the short record on paper from 3.1, free hints, hint-then-show, a missed problem coming back once at the end of its level, and replays at her current stage.

What B gains:

- **It meets the objection as reading 2 states it.** The app breaks every problem into steps and actions to the end, and checks each one as she goes.
- **She still writes every part of the write-up herself**, in the school format, and the plan stays hers. The plan is what the PoC trains.
- **Feedback comes at the action where the mistake happens.** Computer feedback helps less when it comes late (fading-evidence §4). In the closed schedule, from 3.1 on, it came only at the final answer.
- **It is the cheapest of the three.** The build is about the same size as the closed one. The data is the closed list plus about 55 action hints.

What B costs:

- **She solves no problem in the PoC unaided.** By [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md)'s own reading of the fading research (§4), fading leads to unaided paper work "only if the last stage really is unaided". B moves that stage to her homework, which the PoC doesn't see. The parent also loses 4.6–4.7 as the moment to watch her solve alone.
- **She may learn to wait for the app's check after each step.** On homework, nothing checks her between steps. Picking the next step herself in 4.6–4.7 is the only countermeasure inside the app.
- **Level 4 problems get longer** by the taps between steps. A guess: the triangle goes from about 7–9 minutes (written whole, then 8 self-checks) to about 8–11.
- **The notebook stays from Level 2.** If the objection is to the notebook itself (reading 1), B doesn't meet it.
- **The short record and the answer sentence are still checked by her own judgement**, with the model and item-by-item checks, as before.

**If the objection is to the notebook itself (reading 1), take A: on screen to the end.** It is the most game-like option, and the app checks everything itself. But the plan stays a card-ordering task to the end, she never writes the write-up in the PoC, and it costs the most: full guided data for all 30 problems plus word-tile banks, and a mostly new fading build.

**C, write first and check on screen, is the middle option.** The notebook stays, as a switch the parent can turn off, and the guided flow's own menus check each step after she writes it. It fits if the parent wants the notebook but doesn't trust her self-checks. It needs full guided data for all 30 problems.

## 1. What the parent objected to

### What the closed schedule leaves to her alone

| Problems | What she does in the notebook as one block | What the app checks |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1–2.8 | Nothing. The app names each action, she writes it and types its result | Every action |
| 3.1–3.8 | The plan and all the actions | Only the final answer and how many actions she wrote |
| 4.1–4.3 | The short record, the diagram, the plan and all the actions, as headings one at a time (4.3 has nothing prompted) | The decode (4.1, 4.2), then only the final answer and the action count |
| 4.4–4.7 | **The whole write-up from the problem text**, with the card in 4.4–4.5 | The final answer and the action count, then 8 yes/no self-checks |

So in 15 of the 30 problems, a wrong first action only shows up at the final answer, and in 4 she is handed the whole problem at once. The mock showed the first and last of these: the handover at 2.1 («Тепер дії і відповідь ти записуєш у зошит») and the end screen of problem 30, where the triangle is solved entirely in the notebook and then checked against the model with 8 yes/no questions.

### Two readings

**Reading 1: the notebook itself.** She shouldn't be doing the work on paper beside the app, or not this much of it. The objection then starts at 2.1, the first notebook step, and covers 23 problems. The fix is where a faded step goes: it stays on screen (A, or C with the notebook switched off).

**Reading 2: the whole problem at once, with no step structure.** Paper is fine. What's wrong is that the app stops breaking the problem down: from 4.4 she gets the whole problem, and from 3.1 the plan and the actions come as one block, checked only at the end. The fix is to keep the app's one-step-at-a-time structure and its per-action checks to 4.7 (B, or C with the notebook on).

The words lean towards reading 2 ("whole word-problem", "alone", "decompose … step by step"), and the screen the parent reacted to is the whole-problem one. But "in notebook" fits reading 1 too, and both can be true. The grilling decides.

**Reading 2 has one more fork: who breaks the solution into actions.** If the app names every action to the end, which is Level 2's mechanic everywhere, she never plans on her own in the PoC (see [Not proposed](#not-proposed)). B and C keep the plan hers and have the app check it. This is question 2.

## 2. The alternatives

### What all three share

- **The levels and the 30 slots** of [problem-set-plan.md](problem-set-plan.md), and the closed stage boundaries.
- **No problem is handed over whole.** The whole-problem mode, the checklist card and the paper-problem end screen go. Every problem, 4.7 included, is a sequence of steps, one per screen.
- **Actions are checked one by one to the end.** She types each action's result, as in the closed Level 2. In A she also types the action itself.
- **In 4.6–4.7 she picks the next step.** «Який крок далі?» shows the eight step names as buttons. A wrong pick gets «Спершу — Відомо.», and that step opens. Picking a step the problem doesn't have gets «Тут немає порівняння. Який крок далі?». It costs nothing, it's recorded, and it needs no data. It hands over what the card was for: knowing the routine's order without being told.
- **Kept from the closed schedule**: free, recorded hints; hint-then-show on prompted steps; a missed problem (one where the app had to show a result) comes back once at the end of its level; replays run at her current stage.

### A. On screen to the end

The notebook holds only scratch arithmetic, as in the guided flow. Every step stays a screen, and what fades is what each screen gives her. The fading moves from picking to building (word tiles, as in Duolingo's sentence builder, or the keypad), from automatic hints to hints on request, and from built-in hints to none.

| Problems | What changes on screen | What she now produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1–1.7 | Fully guided, as in [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md) | — |
| 2.1–2.4 | **Обчисли**: no chips. She types the action itself (numbers and sign) on a keypad with + − · :, then its result. **Відповідь**: the sentence built from word tiles | Each action; the answer sentence |
| 2.5–2.8 | Slot count hidden in План | How many actions |
| 3.1–3.8 | **Знайти, Відомо**: she builds each short-record line from word tiles, with distractor tiles («Склади рядок зі слів.»). **Тип і схема**: she picks the diagram and places the «?» | The short record, from tiles |
| 4.1–4.3 | **Relations not listed**: she taps each relation's words in the text («Знайди в тексті кожен зв'язок і познач його.»), then names its type. **Перекажи**: a self-question with «Готово» | The relations |
| 4.4–4.5 | **Порівняння**: she builds the restated line from tiles, without «Хто більший?» first. **План**: she types the action count before the cards appear. **Every step**: a wrong try gets «Не так. Спробуй ще раз або натисни «Підказка».» | The decode; the plan's length |
| 4.6–4.7 | She picks each next step | The order of the steps |

- **What she types or taps**: taps, word tiles and the keypad (actions with signs, results, and from 4.4 the action count). Nothing is written on paper.
- **A miss**: up to 4.3, hint then show, as in the guided flow. From 4.4 the hint comes only on «Підказка». A second wrong try still shows the answer, so she never gets stuck. A wrong tile gets a hint that points back at the text, then the line is shown.
- **The plan stays cards to the end.** The app can't check a plan that isn't built from parts, and typed free text was ruled out ([03](../issues/03-poc-tech.md): the keyboard covers half a phone screen, and free text can't be checked).

**Transfer to her homework.** The write-up still builds under the text in the school format, so she sees it 30 times, but she never writes it in the PoC. Her homework is the only paper, and the PoC doesn't see it. CONTEXT.md's Fading ("ending with unaided work on paper") would no longer hold.

**Research.**
- *For*: the app checks every step, so nothing rests on her judging her own work, and children overrate their work (van Loon & Roebers 2017; Lipko et al. 2009, in fading-evidence §3). Step-checked tutors work this way: in MATHia the student does every step with hints on demand, and Cognitive Tutor checks each entry the student builds ([fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) §1).
- *Against*: learners learn most about the steps they are made to produce (Renkl, Atkinson & Große 2004). Generating beats reading, and recall practice beats recognition practice (Bertsch et al. 2007; Rowland 2014; fading-evidence §1). In A the plan, the PoC's main target, never becomes recall. Building from tiles sits between recognition and recall, but nothing in the collected evidence tests it; that placement is my inference.
- *Prompt dependence*: the steps stay prompted to the end, and only the order of the steps is handed over. WWC 2012 warns that too many prompts slow students down and says to remove support gradually ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §1).

**Cost.**
- *Build*: most of [14](../issues/14-build-fading.md) is replaced. Paper steps, self-checks and the card go. New pieces: a word-tile builder with distractors, a keypad with signs and a check of a typed action against the plans (either order for + and ·), tapping relations in the text, «Підказка» on screen steps, the typed action count and the next-step picker. The data types built in [12](../issues/12-build-guided-flow.md) gain tile banks and a support level per step.
- *Data*: every problem needs every step's data, so 30 fully guided problems instead of 15. That means retell menus for 23 problems, plan cards with a distractor for all 30, the decode for every comparison, and relations for all 30. On top come tile banks: the right tiles come from the write-up, but the distractor tiles (the flipped word, the wrong reference, the quantity one action too early) are written per problem, partly from templates. That is roughly double the closed data.

### B. Paper steps one at a time, to the end

The closed schedule up to 4.3, with the plan and actions checked line by line from 3.1, and 4.4–4.7 opened one step at a time instead of whole. The stage boundaries and handover points are the closed ones.

| Problems | The app prompts | Paper steps, one at a time | Plan and actions | Changed from the closed schedule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1–1.7 | All eight steps | — | Plan cards; she builds each action and types its result | Nothing |
| 2.1–2.4 | Перекажи, Знайти, Відомо, Порівняння, Тип і схема, План | Обчисли, Відповідь | The app names each action of her card plan; she writes it and types the result | Nothing |
| 2.5–2.8 | The same | The same | The same, with the slot count hidden | Nothing |
| 3.1–3.8 | Перекажи, Знайти, Порівняння, Тип і схема (she picks the diagram, places the «?») | The short record (after the decode), План, Обчисли, Відповідь | **The plan line by line**, each line checked before the next; **then action by action** | Was: the plan and all the actions in one go, checked only by the final answer and the action count |
| 4.1–4.3 | Порівняння (4.1, 4.2; 4.3 has none) | Everything else, in routine order | **The whole plan, then checked line by line**; then action by action | Was: the same headings, checked only by the final answer and the action count |
| 4.4–4.5 | Nothing | Every step, in routine order | The same | Was: the whole problem at once, with the card |
| 4.6–4.7 | Nothing | Every step; **she picks each next step** | The same | Was: the whole problem at once, no card |

**The plan check.**
- **Level 3, line by line.** «Що знайде перша дія? Запиши в зошит: «1) … — …;»». She taps «Готово». The app shows the valid explanations for that line, taken from all valid plans and their `Order:` swaps in [problem-set.md](problem-set.md): «Яке з цих у тебе?», plus «У мене інакше». In 3.8, for example, the first line can be «швидкість Петра» or «відстань, яку бабуся пройде за 8 хв». With only one valid option, the question becomes «Дія 1 у тебе знаходить ⟨…⟩?» with «Так» / «Ні». At the last line: «Це остання дія: вона знаходить ⟨the asked quantity⟩.»
- **From 4.1, the whole plan.** «Запиши в зошит план: «Розв'язання» і пояснення дій, без обчислень.» Then «Скільки дій у твоєму плані?» on the keypad, then the same line checks, one after another.
- **Then action by action.** «Дія 1: довжина сторони BC. Обчисли в зошиті й введи результат.»
- **The diagram on paper (from 4.1).** She draws it, then taps which diagram she drew («Яка схема в тебе?»: «Схема з відрізками», «Відрізок із дужкою», «Таблиця»). The right one follows from the types, so it needs no data. Then she sees the model and checks the «?».

**4.7, the triangle, under B.** This is the problem the parent saw as a whole-problem end screen:

1. «Який крок далі?» → Перекажи. «Перекажи собі задачу.» → «Готово».
2. → Знайти. «Що треба знайти? Запиши в зошит зі знаком «?».» → the model «P — ?» and its check.
3. → Відомо. «Що відомо? Запиши кожне число з одиницею.» → «AB — 8,4 см» and «Біля кожного числа є одиниця?»
4. → Порівняння. «Є порівняння? Хто більший? Запиши його від шуканого.» → the two restated lines and «Порівняння записане від шуканого: «AC — ?, на 5,1 см більша, ніж BC»?»
5. → Тип і схема. «Назви тип кожного зв'язку. Накресли схему.» → «Яка схема в тебе?» → the model bars and ««?» стоїть там, де шукане?»
6. → План. She writes the skeleton, types 3, then answers «Дія 1 у тебе знаходить довжину сторони BC?» and the same for the other two lines.
7. → Обчисли. She types 12,1, then 17,2, then 37,7, one action at a time.
8. → Відповідь. «Запиши відповідь повним реченням.» → the model and «Відповідь — повним реченням про периметр трикутника?»
9. The ordinary end screen with its «Розбір».

**What she types or taps.**
- **Each action's result, from 2.1 to 4.7.** The last result is the final answer, so there is no separate final-answer entry, and no "where did yours go off" tap: the app knows which action went wrong. In 3.7, the name part of the answer is a pick between the two names.
- **One tap per plan line**, and from 4.1 the action count first.
- **The self-checks**: 2–3 yes/no items per paper part, as in the closed schedule, except the diagram, which is now a pick.
- **The next step** in 4.6–4.7.

**A miss.**
- **Prompted steps**: hint, then show (unchanged).
- **A plan line that isn't valid** («У мене інакше», «Ні», or a wrong action count): «Подивись на короткий запис: що можна знайти з того, що вже відомо?», then the valid line, or lines, with «Виправ у зошиті». She carries on from a valid line. It's recorded as a plan miss on that line.
- **A wrong result**: first «Не сходиться», with the action's hint («Подивись на короткий запис: AC на 5,1 см більша, ніж BC.»). The second time, the action is shown, then «У тебе така сама дія?» with «Так, помилка в обчисленні» / «Ні, інша дія», which keeps arithmetic slips apart from wrong actions. Then «Виправ у зошиті».
- **A paper step's «Ні»**: «Виправ у зошиті» (unchanged). «Підказка» before writing shows the step's self-question, then the model. It never brings back a card, because there isn't one.
- **A wrong next-step pick**: «Спершу — ⟨step⟩», and that step opens.

**Transfer to her homework.** She writes the short record and the plan herself from 3.1, and every part of the write-up from 4.1. From 4.6 she also chooses the order of the steps. Each problem ends with the school write-up in her notebook, in her teacher's format. What she never does in the PoC is a whole problem with no check between the steps. Her homework becomes that stage. Blended products split the work the same way: Zearn's digital lessons break a missed question into smaller steps on screen, students correct their paper notes against the lesson, and each lesson ends with an "un-scaffolded" paper Exit Ticket ([fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) §1). CONTEXT.md's Fading needs rewording (question 9).

**Research.**
- *Recognition vs recall*: every faded step is produced in her own writing, which is the strongest position of the three on "you learn what you are made to produce" (Renkl, Atkinson & Große 2004; Bertsch et al. 2007; Rowland 2014). With more expertise, generating beats studying (Chen, Kalyuga & Sweller 2015). All of these are in fading-evidence §1.
- *Backward fading*: the order is unchanged (actions first, then the short record and the plan, the decode last), with one change per stage. The evidence warns against several steps going to paper at once on the hardest problems, and B keeps that guard.
- *Checking each action*: explaining feedback beats bare answers, and feedback helps less when it comes late (Van der Kleij et al. 2015). Use immediate feedback for difficult tasks and weaker learners (Shute 2008). Fewer errors during learning partly explained why fading worked (Renkl et al. 2002). Catching a wrong action where it happens fits all three; the closed schedule's final-answer check from 3.1 didn't. Sources: fading-evidence §1 and §4.
- *The plan-line check* works like the item-by-item checks that beat whole-model comparison (Lipko et al. 2009; van Loon & Roebers 2017).
- *Apps*: step-checked tutors keep the step structure to the end and add no fading schedule (MATHia). ASSISTments breaks a missed problem into scaffolding questions, one at a time ([fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) §1, §3).
- *Against*: the fading research's last stage is unaided work, and teaching-methods.md reads it as leading to unaided paper work "only if the last stage really is unaided" (§4). WWC 2012's advice to remove support gradually (§1) points the same way. B's last stage still checks every step, so the handover finishes outside the PoC. No study in the collected evidence compares keeping the step checks to the end with removing them.

**Cost.**
- *Build*: most of [14](../issues/14-build-fading.md) stays: paper steps, self-checks, «Підказка», Level 2 results, built-in hints, handovers, replays, missed problems, the records and the stage switch. The whole-problem mode, the card, the final-answer and action-count check from Level 3, and the "where did it go off" tap all go. New pieces: the plan-line check (a tree of valid lines built from each problem's plans and `Order:` lines), action-by-action results from 3.1, the action count from 4.1, the diagram pick after drawing, «У тебе така сама дія?», and the next-step picker. That's about the same size as the closed build.
- *Data*: the closed list (15 problems with full or nearly full guided data, 10 partial, 5 none), plus each action's hint for 3.1–4.7. That's 42 actions in the main plans, plus the other plans' actions that differ (about 15). Optionally, add one order hint per problem for plan misses («Щоб знайти AC, треба вже знати BC»); without it, the generic hint above does the job. The plan-line tree is derived by script, not written. The card's wording is no longer needed.

### C. Write first, then check on screen

Every step stays a guided-flow screen to the end. From 2.1, each step that has a line in the write-up starts in the notebook: she writes it, taps «Готово», and the same step's menu or cards open for her to enter what she wrote, and the app checks it. Steps with no line in the write-up (Перекажи, naming the types) stay screen-only. The notebook part is a switch for the parent. Switched off, C is the guided flow with only the cheap fades below.

| Problems | Written first in the notebook (switch on) | Then checked on screen by | Fades on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1–1.7 | — | The fully guided flow | — |
| 2.1–2.4 | Each action, then the answer sentence | The typed result, as in the closed Level 2; the Відповідь menu | — |
| 2.5–2.8 | The same | The same | Slot count hidden |
| 3.1–3.8 | Also the «?» lines, the given lines (after the decode) and the plan skeleton («Запиши план у зошит, а тоді склади його з карток.») | The Знайти and Відомо menus; the plan cards, ordered to match | She picks the diagram and places the «?» |
| 4.1–4.3 | Also the diagram | Тип і схема | — |
| 4.4–4.5 | Also the restated comparison, before the decode screen | The decode | Hints on request |
| 4.6–4.7 | The same | The same | She picks each next step |

- **What she types or taps**: she writes, then taps the guided flow's menus and cards, and types each result.
- **A miss**: as in the guided flow, hint then show, then «Виправ у зошиті». From 4.4 the hint comes only on «Підказка».

**Transfer to her homework.** She writes every line herself before seeing any options, and the app, not her own judgement, checks each one. Every problem ends with the school write-up in her notebook. Two weaknesses. First, the app checks what she *picks*, not what she *wrote*: after a wrong line she may simply pick the right option, and the notebook keeps the error unless she fixes it. Second, knowing a menu always follows, she may write less carefully. That is my inference, not a finding. Like B, C has no unaided stage, and switched off it has no paper at all.

**Research.**
- *For*: she produces each line first, then gets corrective feedback. Errors followed by corrective feedback help learning, most when the learner was confident (Metcalfe 2017, fading-evidence §4). An objective check avoids children's overrating of their own work (fading-evidence §3).
- *Against*: each step is done twice. [fading-proposal.md](fading-proposal.md) (Decision 3) set aside "the plan written in the notebook first, then entered with cards" for that reason. The menus after writing keep recognition in every step to the end.

**Cost.**
- *Build*: the guided flow from [12](../issues/12-build-guided-flow.md) is reused as the checking screens. New pieces: a write-first prompt per step, the parent switch, the diagram pick, «Підказка» on screen steps and the next-step picker. Most of [14](../issues/14-build-fading.md)'s paper steps and self-checks go. That makes it cheaper to build than A.
- *Data*: full guided menus for all 30 problems: retell, asked, given, decode, relations, plan cards and answer. That is roughly double the closed list, but without A's tile banks. The problems run longest of the three, because each step is written and then entered.

## 3. Questions for the grilling

1. **Which reading?** Is the objection to the notebook (reading 1), to the whole problem at once (reading 2), or to both? Two probes help:
   - Is Level 2 as mocked all right: the app names each action, she writes it in the notebook and types the result?
   - Would 4.7 be all right if the app opened one step at a time and checked each before the next, as in the walk-through above?
2. **Who breaks the solution into actions at the end: the app or her?** If the app names each action to 4.7 (Level 2's mechanic everywhere), she never makes a plan alone in the PoC. If she writes the plan, the app checks it line by line before she computes. Recommended: she writes it, because the plan is the PoC's main target.
3. **The plan on paper: line by line with a check after each line, all the way to the end?** Or line by line in Level 3, then the whole plan checked line by line from 4.1? Recommended: the second.

## Not proposed

- **Level 2's mechanic to the end, where the app names every action and she only writes and computes.** It is the most literal "the app decomposes the problem". But she would never make a plan in the PoC, and the plan is its main target ([01](../issues/01-teaching-methods.md): "the main target for fading"). Question 2 settles it. If the parent chooses it, plan cards are needed for all 30 problems.
- **The whole problem first, with «Розбий на кроки» on request.** This is ASSISTments' pattern, where a wrong answer opens step-by-step scaffolding questions, and close to Zearn's Boost ([fading-apps.md](fading-apps.md) §3–4). It gives one unaided try and never leaves her stuck. But it starts with the whole problem, which is the objection. It could be an optional last try at 4.7 (question 4).
- **Fewer menu options as the fade (three to two).** A lucky guess becomes 1 in 2 instead of 1 in 3, so it removes support in name only. The fade that counts is from picking to producing.
- **Typing the short record or the plan as free text.** The app can't check free text reliably, and the device keyboard covers half a phone screen ([03](../issues/03-poc-tech.md)).
- **Adaptive fading and a stylus.** These were not chosen in the closed schedule, and the objection doesn't touch the reasons.

## Limits

- **Not tried with her, and the parent's reading isn't known yet.** The minutes and screen counts are guesses.
- **No study in the collected evidence compares keeping step checks to the end with removing them**, for 11-year-olds or for word-problem write-ups. The app examples describe products, not trials.
- **"Prompt dependence" is an argued risk, not a measured one here.** It rests on WWC 2012's advice to remove support gradually and on teaching-methods.md's reading of the fading studies.
- **Where word tiles sit between recognition and recall is my inference**, and so is C's "writing less carefully" risk.
- **The action and plan-line counts come from [problem-set.md](problem-set.md)** (42 main-plan actions in 3.1–4.7). The "about 15" other-plan actions is a hand count of the actions that differ from the main plans.
