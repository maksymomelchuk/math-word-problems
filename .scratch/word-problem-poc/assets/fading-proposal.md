# Fading proposal: how the guidance fades across the problem set

Written on 2026-10-03 as the starting point for the parent's grilling of [How does the guidance fade across the problem set?](../issues/08-guidance-fading.md). **It is a proposal, not a decision.** The parent accepts, changes or rejects each recommendation. Nothing here has been tried with her.

Terms are from [CONTEXT.md](../../../CONTEXT.md). The steps are those of [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md): Перекажи, Знайти, Відомо, Порівняння, Тип і схема, План, Обчисли, Відповідь, then the closing Розбір. The levels and slots are from [problem-set-plan.md](problem-set-plan.md).

## In one paragraph

Each level hands one more part of the write-up from the screen to her notebook, from the bottom up, and keeps guided whatever that level makes new. Level 1 is fully guided. In Level 2 she writes the actions and the answer in her notebook and types each result. In Level 3 the short record moves to the notebook as well, the plan loses its slot count, and Знайти and the decode stay guided, because Level 3 brings new question forms and inverted wording. Level 4 goes to paper in three steps. In 4.1–4.2 she writes everything herself and the app shows each part right after she writes it. In 4.3–4.5 she works on paper with a printed checklist card. In 4.6–4.7 she works without the card, which makes the triangle her last problem, solved unaided. This is the backward fading from the teaching-methods research, with the decode faded last. The schedule is fixed by slot, not adaptive. Hints are free. Every faded part keeps a "hint, then show" safety net, so she never gets stuck.

## How to read the per-step modes

A step is in one of these modes in a given problem. This replaces the ticket's "fully guided / prompts only / disappear".

| Mode | What she sees | What she does | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Guided** | The step's screen from [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md): the highlight and the menu or cards | Taps | Hint on the first wrong try, the answer shown on the second |
| **Prompt only** | The step heading and its self-question, with no menu, and the part of the write-up written so far | Writes that part in her notebook (or does it in her head, for Перекажи). Then she taps «Готово» or «Не знаю, покажи» | The app reveals its own version of that part: «Порівняй зі своїм записом. Виправ у зошиті, якщо треба.» A typed result gets a hint, then the right result shown |
| **Paper + card** | The problem text only | Solves on paper with the printed card next to her | She types only the final answer. Help is on request (see Decision 2) |
| **Paper** | The same | The same, without the card | The same |

Neighbouring prompt-only steps share one screen. For example, Відомо and Порівняння together become «Запиши короткий запис».

"Show" for a faded part means revealing the lines of the write-up that the part would have written. That needs no new data.

## Decision 1. At each level, which steps are guided, which become prompts only, and which disappear

### Recommendation

| Step | L1 (1.1–1.7) | L2 (2.1–2.8) | L3 (3.1–3.8) | L4a (4.1–4.2) | L4b (4.3–4.5) | L4c (4.6–4.7) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Перекажи | Guided | Guided | Prompt only (one line on the first screen, nothing revealed) | Prompt only (the same) | Paper + card | Paper |
| Знайти | Guided | Guided | **Guided** (new question forms) | Prompt only | Paper + card | Paper |
| Відомо | Guided | **Guided** (hidden information) | Prompt only: short record in the notebook | Prompt only | Paper + card | Paper |
| Порівняння | Guided | Guided | **Guided** (inverted wording; fades last) | Prompt only | Paper + card | Paper |
| Тип і схема | Guided | Guided | Guided, but she picks the diagram | Prompt only: she draws it | Paper + card | Paper |
| План | Guided | Guided | Guided, but she thinks first and gets no slot count | Prompt only | Paper + card | Paper |
| Обчисли | Guided | Prompt only: actions in the notebook, each result typed | The same | Prompt only: final answer typed | Paper: final answer typed | Paper: final answer typed |
| Відповідь | Guided | Prompt only: full sentence in the notebook | The same | Prompt only | Paper + card | Paper |
| Розбір | Full | Full | Full | Full | Full + self-check | Full + self-check |

Bold marks a step kept guided because that level makes it new.

**Built-in hints**, which fade on their own (from the ticket's list, plus three more):

| Built-in hint | L1 | L2 | L3 | L4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three-option menus | Every guided step | Every guided step | Only in Знайти, Порівняння and Тип і схема | None |
| Relations listed for her in Тип і схема | Yes | Yes | Yes | No: she finds them herself |
| Diagram family chosen for her | Yes | Yes | **No**: «Яку схему накреслиш?» (Схема з відрізками / Відрізок із дужкою / Таблиця), then the pre-drawn diagram | She draws it |
| Number of slots in План | Yes | Yes | **No**: she taps «План готовий» when she's done | No cards |
| Distractor card in План | 1 | 1 | 1 | — |
| «Чому?» (at most one per problem) | Every problem: each brings in a new type | Only 2.1, 2.3, 2.4 and 2.7, the problems with hidden information | Only 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.4 and 3.8, the first time she meets each inverted form | None |
| Highlight in the text | Yes | Yes | Only for guided steps | No |
| Checklist | The step headings on screen | The same | The same | On-screen headings (4a), printed card (4b), none (4c) |

**Why, level by level:**

- **L1, fully guided.** This is the research's stage A ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md), "Fading across a 20-problem set"). Level 1 is grouped on purpose, to introduce the type names ([problem-set-plan.md](problem-set-plan.md)). One relation per problem keeps the full routine short. After each problem the Розбір shows the whole write-up, which works as a worked example. One «Чому?» per problem follows stage A's "1–2 menu 'why?' prompts", kept at one because of the Barbieri 2023 caveat.
- **L2, compute and answer hand over first.** This is backward fading: the last steps go first ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §4, Renkl & Atkinson 2003). With 8th graders, calculation gained most from backward fading (Große 2015, same section). She already calculates well (map Notes), so this is the safest step to hand over first. It also starts the notebook habit early, so paper is not a cliff in Level 4. Level 2 brings in hidden information, which sits in Відомо, so Відомо stays guided. The action-building chips go. Her typed results still put each mistake on the action where it happens.
- **L3, the short record moves to the notebook; Знайти and the decode stay guided.** Her teacher requires the short record ([guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md)), and the fading has to get her producing it (ticket). Starting in L3 gives her 8 reps with a reveal before paper, not 0. Inverted wording first appears in L3, and the research fades the decode last, so Порівняння stays guided for every comparison in L3, including the four inverted ones (3.1, 3.2, 3.4, 3.8). L3 also brings question-first, instruction and two-part questions, which are Знайти's job, so Знайти stays guided. The plan, the main target for fading ([01](../issues/01-teaching-methods.md)), loses its slot count. Picking the diagram family is the first decision she'll have to make on paper.
- **L4a, headings only with feedback on each part (4.1–4.2).** This is the research's stage C: "only the step headings remain … feedback only on request". Both problems carry an inverted link, so she decodes on her own twice, with a reveal right after, before paper.
- **L4b, paper with the card (4.3–4.5).** Stage D: paper with a printed checklist card. The app takes the final answer and a self-check.
- **L4c, the card is removed (4.6–4.7).** Stage D: "The card goes away for the last 2–3 problems." The triangle closes the set unaided, on paper, with its inverted link. The paper checks were dropped (map, Out of scope), so 4.6–4.7 in her notebook are the closest thing left to an "after" check for the parent.

### Alternatives

- **A. The research's four stages, one per level** (L1 guided, L2 backward fading, L3 headings only, L4 paper). It's the simplest mapping, and the 20-problem suggestion scales to 30 almost exactly. But L3 is where inverted wording first appears. With headings only there, she meets the trap with no guided decode at all, which breaks "decode last" in practice. Not recommended for that reason.
- **B. Everything stays on screen until Level 4.** L2–L3 drop only the chips and the answer menu, she types results, and nothing is written in a notebook. It's more game-like and shorter in L2–L3, which helps *is it interesting*. But L4 becomes a cliff: the short record, decode, diagram and plan all become recall at once, on the hardest problems, with only 7 short-record reps. This is the main trade-off for the parent: how much notebook writing she'll accept inside a game.
- **C. Open each level with a worked example** (a "shown" mode, where each screen arrives with the right answer picked, plus «Далі»). It's closer to the research's stage A. With 8th graders, translating a situation into maths gained most from example–problem pairs (Große 2015, [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §4). Costs: a shown mode to build, and four slots lose their practice rep. The fully guided problem is already menu-prompted, close to the menu-based prompting in Atkinson et al. 2003, and the Розбір shows the full write-up each time. Risk: watching is the least game-like thing in the set.
- **D. All of Level 4 on paper** (card on 4.1–4.4, none on 4.5–4.7). It needs no L4a mode and leaves three unaided problems. But her first chain on paper gets no feedback on its parts, only on the final answer.
- **E. Fewer «Чому?»:** only at the L3 decode, the trap. It's shortest and most focused. Self-explanation prompts added to backward fading improved far transfer (Renkl & Atkinson 2003), but adding them to worked examples went with smaller effects (Barbieri 2023). Both are in [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §4.

## Decision 2. Can she ask for a hint, and do hints cost anything?

### Recommendation

- **Guided steps: no hint button.** Keep the rule from [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md): a hint after the first wrong try, and the answer shown after the second. On a three-option menu, a hint button is cheaper for her than thinking, so she would learn to tap it first.
- **Prompt-only parts: the reveal is the help.** «Не знаю, покажи» reveals that part early. A typed result that's wrong gets the action's hint from the data, then the right action is shown. After a shown action she gets one tap, «У тебе така сама дія?», answered «Так, помилилась в обчисленні» or «Ні, у мене інша дія». That keeps arithmetic slips apart from wrong actions, as the chips did.
- **Paper (L4b–c): help on request.** She taps «Я застрягла», then «Що показати?»: «Що треба знайти», «Короткий запис», «План» or «Розв'язання». Each option reveals that part of the write-up and is recorded against its step. A wrong final answer gets «Не сходиться. Перевір план і дії.» The second wrong answer shows the full write-up with «Знайди, де в тебе інакше, і виправ у зошиті», the school's «робота над помилками».
- **Hints cost nothing in the game.** No hearts, XP or stars are lost. The only "cost" is that every hint and every shown part is recorded per step, as already decided. A costly hint pushes her to guess on three-option menus, or to hide that she's stuck.

### Alternatives

- **A hint button on guided steps too.** There would be fewer wrong taps, which matters if [Which Duolingo-style game mechanics go into the PoC?](../issues/09-game-mechanics.md) makes a wrong answer cost hearts. It invites skipping the thinking.
- **Hints cost something**, such as fewer stars for a problem done with help. That pushes against overusing hints. Cost: more guessing, and a child who avoids help when she needs it. *(The help-avoidance point comes from general tutoring research, not the closed tickets. Unverified here.)*
- **No help on paper at all**, a pure test. It gives cleaner evidence for the verdict, but she can get stuck, which breaks the "she never gets stuck" principle in [guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md).

## Decision 3. When does she first produce the plan herself, and in what form?

### Recommendation

The plan moves to her in three steps:

1. **L3: she thinks first, then enters the plan with cards.** «План. Подумай: скільки дій потрібно і що знайде кожна? Коли план готовий, склади його з карток.» The cards appear only after she taps «План готовий». There's no slot count, and one distractor stays. The app still checks the plan and gives its order hints («Щоб знайти AC, треба вже знати BC…»). Every valid plan is accepted. The first moment of producing the plan is in her head; the cards are only how she enters it.
2. **L4a: written in her notebook,** as the explanations after each dash. Then the plan is revealed, with every valid plan shown: «Будь-який із цих планів правильний.»
3. **L4b–c: on paper,** inside the school write-up: «1) … = … (см) — довжина сторони BC;». On paper the plan is the explanations after the dashes ([ukrainian-grade6-word-problems.md](ukrainian-grade6-word-problems.md) §5). The parent can read her plan from them, the same way [paper-checks.md](paper-checks.md) scored Plan.

### Alternatives

- **Typed.** Not recommended. The keypad has only digits ([03](../issues/03-poc-tech.md)). The phone's own keyboard covers half the screen. Free text can't be checked.
- **The slot count goes already in L2.** It's cheap, and L2 plans have only two actions. But it fades the plan in the same level as compute, against the backward order.
- **In L3, the plan written in the notebook first, then entered with cards.** That makes it real recall before the cards, at the cost of writing the plan twice. Also, on paper the school writes no separate plan line.
- **First production only on paper, in L4.** It's the simplest build, but she gets no checked plan practice without the slot-count crutch.

## Decision 4. What happens when she loops back through a problem she has already solved?

### Recommendation

- **A replay uses the support of the furthest stage she has reached, not the problem's original one.** For example, replaying 1.4 after she has started L3 runs it in L3 mode. Seen menus are remembered answers, so replaying them trains recognition of her own earlier picks, not planning. Lighter support also keeps a replay short.
- **Once she has finished the set,** every replay runs in paper mode (L4c), with «Я застрягла» available. Her answer to an earlier problem may be remembered. On a replay what counts is the write-up in her notebook, not the number.
- **Whether there's a second full pass, and in what order,** is for the trial (map, Not yet specified: how many days). If there is one, a mixed order across all 30 fits the interleaving research (Rohrer et al. 2020, [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §6).
- **The first pass goes in slot order.** The schedule assumes she meets the problems in the order of [problem-set-plan.md](problem-set-plan.md).

### Alternatives

- **Replay exactly as the first time.** It's simplest and predictable. Problems get longer than she now needs, and she clicks through remembered menus.
- **One stage lighter than the first time.** It's gentler for problems she struggled with, but harder to explain and build.
- **Replays offered only for problems with a shown answer** ("practise your mistakes"). This is mostly a game-layer choice for [09](../issues/09-game-mechanics.md); it fits on top of either rule above.

## Decision 5. Paper or stylus for the last stage, and how she enters a paper result

### Recommendation

- **Paper, in a thin squared notebook (зошит у клітинку) kept only for the trial.** It's what her homework asks for, which is the ticket's destination. Drawing her own segments or table is natural on paper. It works whichever device is her main one, and it costs nothing to build. The notebook from Level 2 on is also a record the parent can read when judging *does it help*.
- **Entering a paper result:** she types the final number on the keypad, with the unit shown. The entry comes after she has finished on paper, so showing the unit can't steer her work. A two-part question gets two fields. Then the Розбір: the full write-up, the 2–3 checks, the key idea and the other plan. Then a self-check of four taps, «Перевір свій запис», each answered «Так» or «Ні»:
  - «Є короткий запис, шукане — зі знаком «?»»
  - «Порівняння записане від шуканого» (only when the problem has one)
  - «Після кожної дії є пояснення»
  - «Відповідь — повним реченням»

  These follow [paper-checks.md](paper-checks.md), which scored understanding apart from the final answer, and stage D's "the app takes only the final answer and a self-check".
- **The card is printed,** about A6, and lies next to the notebook. For 4.6 the app says «Далі — без картки. Відклади її.» Its headings are the app's own step names, so the card is the list she has seen on screen for 23 problems. It follows the school skeleton: short record, then Розв'язання 1), 2) …, then Відповідь ([ukrainian-grade6-word-problems.md](ukrainian-grade6-word-problems.md) §5). It puts the decode before the diagram, because an accurate diagram goes with ~99% correct answers on inverted problems and an inaccurate one with ~1.4% (de Koning 2022, [teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §3). Draft:

> **Як я розв'язую задачу**
>
> 1. **Перекажи.** Прочитай задачу. Про що вона? Скажи собі одним реченням.
> 2. **Знайти.** Що треба знайти? Підкресли запитання. Запиши шукане зі знаком «?».
> 3. **Відомо.** Запиши кожне число з назвою та одиницею. Одиниці однакові? Якщо ні — перетвори: 1 год = 60 хв, 1 кг = 1000 г, 1 км = 1000 м.
> 4. **Порівняння.** Шукане більше чи менше за дане? Запиши порівняння від шуканого: «Друге число — ?, на 4 більше, ніж перше».
> 5. **Тип і схема.** Який тип у кожного зв'язку? Накресли схему з відрізками або таблицю.
> 6. **План.** Що знайде кожна дія? Остання дія знаходить те, що питають.
> 7. **Обчисли.** Запиши по діях: «1) … = … (одиниця) — що ти знайшла цією дією;».
> 8. **Відповідь.** Повним реченням: «Відповідь: …».

The example in line 4 is deliberately not from the problem set. Check it against `problem-set.md` once that is written. The card must not give away any Level 4 decode.

### Alternatives

- **Stylus on a tablet.** Web Pointer Events support it ([03](../issues/03-poc-tech.md)). It only works if her main device is a tablet with a stylus, and that device isn't named yet. Finger drawing on a phone is slow and imprecise ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §3, phone fit). It needs a drawing canvas and image storage beyond `localStorage`. The app still can't read it, and it isn't the notebook her homework uses. Its one gain: her work is kept with each attempt.
- **Intermediate results typed as well as the final answer.** The app could say which action went wrong, but only if she followed a plan the data lists, and on paper the app doesn't know her plan.
- **A photo of the notebook page** saved with the attempt. It's evidence for the verdict without the parent sitting there. Whether to log attempts at all is the trial's call (map, Not yet specified).
- **The card on screen,** or **she copies the card into the notebook's first page.** On screen needs no printing, but on a phone it competes with the problem text. Copying it is memorable, but costs a session.

## Decision 6. A fixed fading schedule, or fading a step once she has done it right twice?

### Recommendation

**A fixed schedule by slot, as in the Decision 1 table, with the "hint, then show" safety net on every faded part.**

- **Predictable.** The parent knows what each problem asks of her, and every problem's length is known in advance.
- **It fits the levels.** Each level adds one difficulty. A step that fades early on a streak would lose support just as the next level makes it hard again. For example, the decode would fade after two plain comparisons in L1–L2, right before inverted wording arrives in L3.
- **Cheaper data.** [Write the guided-step data for the problem set](../issues/13-write-guided-data.md) writes data only for the steps a problem prompts. Under adaptive fading, any step could stay guided anywhere, so every in-app problem needs full menus. The retell, given and answer menus are the costly ones: every wrong option has to be a real misreading.
- **"Two right" is a weak signal on three-option menus.** Two lucky guesses in a row happen about one time in nine.
- **The record is kept anyway.** Hints, shown answers and slips per step are still recorded, so the parent can see where she leans on help.

### Alternatives

- **B. Fade a step after two right first tries** (no hint, no show). It would be counted per step, per type for Тип і схема, and per inverted form for the decode. The research left open whether this beats a fixed schedule ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md), end of "Recommended step sequence"). One study since then supports adaptive fading. Salden, Aleven, Schwonke & Renkl (2010), with high-school geometry students in one lab and one classroom experiment, found adaptive fading better than fixed fading, which was better than no examples. Adaptive fading was strongest on delayed transfer. Caveats: their signal was a tutor's estimate built from the quality of the students' self-explanations, per geometry principle, not a two-right counter. The students were older, and the topic was geometry. *(Read at abstract level for this proposal; not in the closed tickets.)* Costs: full step data for all in-app problems, unpredictable lengths, and per-type counters to build.
- **C. A hybrid hold-back.** Fixed schedule, but a cheap support that's due to go stays for one more problem if she needed a show on it last time. The cheap supports are the slot count, the diagram-family pick, Обчисли and Відповідь, whose data comes from the write-up. It's adaptive where the data is cheap, and it keeps the "fade only after two right" spirit. Its cost: L2–L3 problems need answer menus and chips data too, and in L4 the card's removal must stay fixed, or the unaided 4.6–4.7 stop being unaided.

## Length, level by level (guesses, not tried)

The parent accepted about 15 screens, or 4–6 minutes, for the fully guided triangle ([guided-flow.md](guided-flow.md)). Fading shortens the screens. It doesn't necessarily shorten the minutes, because writing in the notebook takes the time back.

| Level | Screens per problem | Writing in the notebook |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | ~11–13 | Scratch arithmetic only |
| L2 | ~12–14 | Two actions and the answer sentence |
| L3 | ~10–12 | Short record, actions, answer |
| L4a | ~6–7 | The whole write-up and a diagram |
| L4b–c | ~3–4 | The whole write-up and a diagram |

Roughly 2–3 hours for one pass. That's a guess, for the trial to plan around.

## Questions for the grilling

1. **Notebook from Level 2 (recommended) or screen-only until Level 4 (Alt. B)?** Fun against a gentle road to recall. The parent knows how much writing she'll take inside a game.
2. **Level 4 split as 2 + 3 + 2 (recommended), or all on paper (Alt. D)?** And is the triangle unaided the right ending?
3. **Fixed schedule (recommended), two-right adaptive (B) or the hybrid hold-back (C)?**
4. **Hints free (recommended)?** This has to line up with what a wrong answer costs in [09](../issues/09-game-mechanics.md).
5. **Paper notebook (recommended) or stylus?** Which device is her main one?
6. **Does her teacher accept a segment diagram or table in place of the line short record?** That decides whether the card makes the diagram optional.
7. **«Чому?»:** the first-meeting rule (~16 prompts) or the decode only (Alt. E)?
8. **Worked examples (Alt. C):** none, or one at the start of each level?

## Limits

- **Not tried with her.** The screen counts and times are guesses.
- **Self-reports are self-reports.** The «Так / Ні» self-check and «У тебе така сама дія?» rely on an 11-year-old judging her own notebook. The notebook itself is the reliable record.
- **The research is about other ages and tasks.** No fading study found with 10–12-year-olds is aimed at word-problem planning ([teaching-methods.md](teaching-methods.md) §4). The four stages here adapt a 20-problem suggestion that was never tested.
- **Salden et al. 2010 was read at abstract level only.**
