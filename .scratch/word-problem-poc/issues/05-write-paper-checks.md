## Question

Write paper check A (before) and paper check B (after), with 3–4 Ukrainian word problems each.

- Pair the problems up: each problem in B matches one in A in type and difficulty, so B is a fair re-test of A
- Draw them from the problem types chosen for the problem set, but don't reuse any problem from the set itself
- Include a scoring sheet for the parent that scores *understanding* separately from *arithmetic*: did she find what was asked, was her plan/order right, did she avoid wording traps, was the final answer right. The PoC trains the first three, so the verdict depends on those
- Provide a printable sheet for each check, plus the worked answers
- Give each check at least one "less than … by" trap problem, one rate problem and one multi-step problem. The PoC is a short dose compared with the studies (10–29 lessons), so the checks have to target exactly what it trains for a change to show. See [Which word-problem teaching methods work for kids her age?](01-teaching-methods.md)

Use the school's format and wording from [How does Ukrainian 6th grade expect word problems to be solved on paper?](02-ukrainian-school-format.md). Score her plan from her step explanations (the text after each dash). Don't penalise a missing short record, since teachers differ on it.

AFK: the agent writes it, the parent reviews it. Output goes in `../assets/`.

From [Which problem types and difficulty ramp does the problem set cover?](04-problem-types-and-ramp.md): draw the problems from its six problem types, and follow its number rules (whole numbers by default; decimals only with + and −) and the «ти» form. The walking boy and the triangle are in the problem set itself, as slots 2.3 and 4.7 of [problem-set-plan.md](../assets/problem-set-plan.md), so the checks use matched variants of them. The plan's levels give the difficulty to pair against.

## Comments

### Resolution

Checks: [paper-checks.md](../assets/paper-checks.md), which has the pairs, the rules for running a check, the worked answers, the scoring guide and the score tables. Her printable sheets: [paper-check-a.html](../assets/paper-check-a.html) and [paper-check-b.html](../assets/paper-check-b.html). The parent approved them on 2026-10-03.

**Answer.**

- **Four matched pairs.** Each B problem keeps its A problem's relations, wording, level and number kinds; only the story and the numbers change.
  1. на + частини і ціле, with the reference left out (Level 2)
  2. A variant of the walking boy: три величини twice, with 1 год = 60 хв (Level 2)
  3. у разів (*inverted*) + частини і ціле (Level 3)
  4. A variant of the triangle: на + на (*inverted*) + частини і ціле, with decimal + (Level 4)

  Problem 4 is the "less than … by" problem and the multi-step one; problem 2 is the rate problem. Problem 3 is a second inverted relation, so the Decode score doesn't rest on one problem.
- **Scoring, per problem.** Asked (0–1), Plan (0–2) and Decode (0–1) measure understanding, out of 16 per check. Answer (0–1) is scored separately, out of 4. Plan is read from her explanations after each dash, and it judges the route, not how she read the tricky relation, which is Decode's job. Each problem lists its usual wrong answers and how to score them. A missing short record isn't penalised.
- **Running a check.** The same rules apply to A and B: one sitting, no calculator, no help, and the same opening words. Afterwards the parent asks about any step without an explanation and records her answer. **Don't go over check A with her until check B is done**, because that would teach the four pairs outside the PoC.

**Limits.** Four of the six types are tested: there's no дріб від числа, no зближення and no question-first wording. The matching is a judgement, not a measurement.
